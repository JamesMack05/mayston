*Captured-position context (from the STATE-2 elicitation that fed this close): Bobath-with-caveats, movement-observation valid, superiority claim dropped — in a room with one strong tutor-track defender. The brief below is calibrated to that position.*

## Key claims
- Bobath is **not superior** to other approaches overall, and is **inferior to task-specific training for lower-limb activities** (SMD 0.48, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.95; Scrivener et al. 2020). Quote the conclusion as written — *"Prioritising Bobath therapy over other interventions is not supported by current evidence"* — not "facilitation doesn't work."
- The result is **method-level**: it speaks to Bobath-as-intervention, **not** to Bobath-as-movement-observation-framework — a distinction no SR has tested. Your caveated position lives exactly on this seam, and it is defensible because the seam is real.
- No SR shows Bobath inferior on *every* outcome to *every* comparator (Kollen et al. 2009: *"this systematic review confirms that overall the Bobath Concept is not superior to other approaches… no evidence is available for the superiority of any approach"*; one Wang orthopedic-vs-Bobath trial favoured Bobath on MAS/SIS; Pollock et al. 2014: Bobath superior to PNF for standing balance). State the direction-of-effect honesty out loud before your tutor-track colleague produces the counter-trial.
- UK guidelines (NICE NG236, RCP/ISWP 2023) name *no* approach; all three of NICE / RCP / ESO 2023 recommend task-specific / repetitive task training (Olaoye, Lokesh, Aboderin & Ojo 2024, Healthcare 12:1433). RCP/ISWP positions RTT as *"the principal rehabilitation method alongside traditional exercise."* The guideline level resolves on the intervention; it stays silent on the framework.

## Evidence certainty
- *Task-specific training beats Bobath for lower-limb activities (Scrivener et al. 2020):* **moderate quality** — task-specific training **probably** produces better lower-limb outcomes than Bobath. *Cochrane-SoF-style footnote: certainty downgraded to moderate quality for risk of bias and imprecision across the contributing trials — included-trial PEDro range 2–8/10, lower CI bound 0.01 nearly touching the null.* The honest sentence is "task-specific training probably produces better lower-limb outcomes than Bobath," not "Bobath is debunked."
- *Bobath not superior to combined interventions or strength training (Scrivener et al. 2020; Kollen et al. 2009; Pathak et al. 2021):* **moderate quality** for the "not superior" body of evidence — a robust, repeated SR-level finding across roughly 16 years. *Cochrane-SoF footnote: downgraded for risk of bias and imprecision across the contributing trials.*
- *Bobath superior to PNF for standing balance (Pollock et al. 2014):* **low quality** — Bobath **may** improve standing balance relative to PNF; a single-comparison finding, not replicated across the SR base.
- *Bobath-as-framework is a valid clinical-reasoning / movement-observation lens:* **very low quality** as an *empirical* claim — the effect is **very uncertain** because no intervention trial tests it. It is a theoretical / clinical-utility position (Mayston's 2008 reframe; Penny's movement-observation defence), not an evidence-validated one. Do not present it as graded clinical evidence; present it as the un-refuted framework seam.

## Contested areas
- **Vested interest — High signal, cuts both ways** (`reference/schools/bobath.md § Vested-interest signal` — the strongest of the six schools). Your tutor-track colleague holds structural course-delivery / credential / reputational stakes in Bobath surviving (tutor status described in the source as "a very lucrative place to be") — this is structural, not a personal accusation. Critics can hold the mirror-image stake: Sarah Tyson frames the schools as *"cults rather than applied science"* and notes *"many have invested time, effort, finances and reputation on the named approaches… vested interests in keeping them going"* — and critics themselves carry non-commercial stakes too: reputation and career investment in the competing evidence-based paradigm. Name both halves in the room; that lets you say "everyone here has a stake, mine included" rather than appearing to ambush the defender.
- **The framework-vs-treatment seam** — the genuinely unresolved fault line, and the crux of the whole night. The intervention evidence cannot refute the framework claim, because SRs test interventions, not frameworks; the two positions are not arguing about the same object. Margaret Mayston's 2008 *Physiotherapy Research International* editorial (a Senior Bobath Tutor, so a defender) argues for Bobath as *"a contributor to client-based neurorehabilitation, not the leading actor who wishes to be centre stage at all times."* Hold both layers open; do not collapse them into one verdict.

## Verify before you present
- **PEDro** — look up the included-trial quality scores directly and confirm the 2–8/10 spread before anyone argues "the trials were high quality." Load-bearing distinction to hold in the room: PEDro rates the methodological quality of a *single* RCT (items 2–11, 0–10); GRADE rates the certainty of a *body* of evidence across trials. They are different axes — a 10/10 PEDro trial can still sit inside a moderate-quality or low-quality evidence base. A high single-trial PEDro score does not upgrade the pooled GRADE certainty.
- **PubMed** — pull Scrivener et al. 2020's included-studies table to see exactly which trials drive the SMD 0.48, then look each one up on PEDro. PubMed is free and needs no OpenAthens (relevant if you are between NHS contracts — CINAHL via OpenAthens may be unavailable; PubMed and PEDro are both free and need no Athens). Whatever the tools return, you read the primary trials yourself — this routes you to the source; it does not retrieve for you.
