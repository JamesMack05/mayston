# Carr & Shepherd / Motor Relearning Programme (MRP)

The cleanest task-oriented counterpoint to Bobath. The SR finding that MRP-family task-specific training *beats* Bobath for lower-limb activities is the build's gold differentiator. Read alongside `reference/debates/task-specific-vs-eclectic.md`.

## Founder

Janet Carr and Roberta Shepherd, Australian physiotherapists.

## Origin

Developed in the late 1980s; the canonical paper "A Motor Learning Model for Stroke Rehabilitation" (Carr) was published 1989. Australia-origin (Sydney). UK NHS adoption is widespread — UK private clinic chains (e.g. physio.co.uk) list MRP as a routine neurological-rehabilitation approach, and its principles align with current UK guideline direction.

## Core theoretical claim

Stroke rehabilitation is a **motor-relearning process** driven by task-specific, goal-oriented practice. The brain is dynamic and capable of reorganisation after CNS injury (an early neuroplasticity-aware framework). Treatment principles:

- Treatment is task-oriented — real ADLs (sit-to-stand, walking, reach, grasp).
- Active patient participation; verbal and visual cues; cognitive engagement.
- Use of the affected side; discourage compensatory movement with the unaffected side.
- Prevent muscle shortening (a spasticity precursor).
- Treatment in a stimulating, functional, context-specific environment.
- Four steps: analyse essential task components → practise the missing components → practise the whole task → transfer to the real environment.
- Manual facilitation techniques are de-emphasised; verbal instruction, demonstration and guidance are emphasised.

This places MRP firmly on the **task-oriented** side of fault line 1 (facilitation-based vs task-oriented), with theory roots in motor-learning research (Schmidt, Lee) and neuroplasticity literature.

## Evidence stance

Favourable, with the strongest support sitting at the comparative-SR level. GRADE-calibrated: moderate-to-good for the task-specific principle generally; individual MRP RCTs are small.

- **Scrivener et al. 2020**: task-specific training (the MRP family) significantly outperforms Bobath therapy for lower-limb activities (SMD 0.48, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.95). This is the SR finding MRP/task-oriented advocates cite.
- **Veerbeek et al. 2014** (cited within Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015): *"the effectiveness of most interventions for the upper and lower limbs is driven by repetition and principles of task- and context-specific motor learning."*
- **Alfaleh et al. 2025** (J Comparative Effectiveness Research): prospective single-blinded multicentre RCT, Saudi Arabia, n=32 chronic stroke. MRP + conventional PT vs conventional PT alone, 18 sessions over 6 weeks. Large effect sizes — Barthel Index (Cohen's d = 1.46), Motor Assessment Scale sit-to-stand (r = −0.865). Conclusion: *"MRP significantly improves sit-to-stand transfer and ADL performance in chronic stroke patients."* **Limitations (flag explicitly):** small sample (n=32), baseline MAS imbalance.
- **Ghrouz et al. 2022** (Eur Stroke J): RCT protocol only (n=66 sub-acute stroke, NCT05076383); results pending at search date — note as an open/unresolved trial.

## UK named defenders

No UK-specific named-tutor body. The named defenders are the founders themselves — **Janet Carr** and **Roberta Shepherd** (still publishing into the 2010s, e.g. the textbook "Stroke Rehabilitation: Guidelines for Exercise and Training to Optimise Motor Skill") — plus the broad contemporary motor-learning research community. In the UK, MRP's position is carried more by guideline alignment than by a named credentialing body.

## UK named critics

Less polarised than Bobath — there is no UK named-critic faction. Critique is mostly **methodological**: Alfaleh 2025's small sample; older trials of varied methodological quality (PEDro 2–8/10 across the Scrivener Bobath SR comparators). No politicised UK opposition exists.

## Vested-interest signal

**Low.** MRP has no named-course-tutor commercial credentialing structure equivalent to Bobath's tutor system — there is no "MRP Tutor" credential. This absence is itself worth naming when the educator weighs why one school attracts more heat than another.

## UK NHS uptake

Implicitly endorsed by current EBP/task-specific guidance. NICE NG236 (§1.13.20 Repetitive Task Training) and the RCP/ISWP 2023 National Clinical Guideline for Stroke both recommend task-specific/repetitive task training in approach-agnostic, intervention-named language that aligns with MRP principles, even though they do not name "Carr-Shepherd" or "MRP" explicitly.

## Debate-mode handling

- **Place it on fault line 1 as task-oriented** before debating particulars.
- **Use the Scrivener 2020 comparative finding** (task-specific > Bobath for lower-limb activities) as the anchor — but state it precisely: the evidence says "task-specific training beats Bobath where directly compared," NOT "facilitation doesn't work."
- **Flag the MRP-specific RCT limitations** (Alfaleh small sample + baseline imbalance; Ghrouz results pending) so the educator does not over-claim from individual trials — the strong evidence is at the task-specific-principle level, not the single-MRP-trial level.
- **Name the low vested-interest signal** as a contrast with Bobath when the educator is weighing why the debate is heated.
