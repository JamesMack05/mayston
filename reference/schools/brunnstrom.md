# Brunnstrom Movement Therapy

A school where the honest position is a split verdict: the **staging framework is alive and embedded in mainstream assessment**, but the **therapy-as-intervention has thin direct evidence**. Holding both halves is the source-weighing behaviour the build is built to demonstrate. Read alongside `reference/debates/brunnstrom-stages-vs-therapy.md`.

## Founder

Signe Brunnstrom, Swedish physical therapist.

## Origin

Developed in the 1960s, primarily in the US after Brunnstrom emigrated. Canonical reference text: "Movement Therapy in Hemiplegia." Sweden-origin / US-developed. The **Brunnstrom Stages** framework is used worldwide even where the therapy itself is not practised.

## Core theoretical claim

Recovery from stroke proceeds in **reverse development** — reflexes resurface first, then subcortical synergy patterns, then progress to voluntary, isolated movement. Treatment intentionally *uses* reflexes, associated reactions, and proprioceptive/exteroceptive stimuli to elicit motion at early stages, then progressively de-emphasises them as voluntary control returns. The seven-stage recovery sequence:

1. flaccidity
2. synergy emerging
3. synergy at peak / spasticity
4. movement out of synergy
5. complex movement combinations
6. coordinated isolated joint movement
7. normal motor function

This places Brunnstrom on the **facilitation-based** side of fault line 1. Its foundational reflex-hierarchical motor-control model is the same 1960s neurophysiology that contemporary motor-learning theory has largely superseded.

## Evidence stance

A deliberate split — flag both halves explicitly. GRADE-calibrated: framework utility is well-established; therapy-as-intervention is *very uncertain* (sparse direct evidence).

- **Therapy as intervention — thin.** Physiopedia notes explicitly: *"There are several methods on rehabilitation of stroke patients however very little evidence is present"* for Brunnstrom therapy. No major contemporary SR specifically on Brunnstrom therapy as a treatment was located — this is an absence-of-evidence gap, distinct from evidence-of-absence.
- **One small RCT favours it for chronic hand recovery — name the limits.** Pandian, Arya & Davidson 2011 (J Bodywork and Movement Therapies, PMID 22703742): RCT n=30 chronic post-stroke. Brunnstrom Hand Manipulation (BHM, "uses synergies and reflexes") vs MRP hand protocol. Both effective (BRS-H p=0.003–0.004; FMA-WH p<0.001). BHM > MRP for FMA-WH (p<0.004) and FMA item VIII hand motor recovery (p<0.033). Conclusion: *"BHM was found to be more effective than MRP in rehabilitation of the hand in chronic post-stroke patients."* **Limitations (load-bearing):** small sample (n=30, 15/arm); single-centre Indian rehabilitation institute (generalisability to UK NHS not established); the BRS-H outcome measure derives from the Brunnstrom framework itself (potential measurement bias toward detecting changes the approach is designed to elicit). Not replicated within the retrievable literature.
- **Stages framework — robust.** The Fugl-Meyer Assessment (FMA), the canonical motor-impairment scale, derives its seven-stage motor-recovery structure directly from the Brunnstrom framework. Gladstone et al. 2002 (Neurorehabil Neural Repair, PMID 12234086): *"the Fugl-Meyer motor scale is recommended highly as a clinical and research tool for evaluating changes in motor impairment."* Hsieh et al. 2016 worked explicitly to "improve the utility of the Brunnstrom recovery stages" — confirming the staging tool persists even where the therapy does not.

## UK named defenders

No UK-specific named defenders and no UK named-tutor body. Brunnstrom's name persists via the canonical textbook and the Stages framework rather than via any credentialing institution. There is no contemporary UK faction defending Brunnstrom therapy as a primary intervention.

## UK named critics

No named UK critic faction. The objection is **theoretical, not personal**: the reflex-hierarchical motor-control model the approach assumes is contested at the foundational level (Levin & Piscitelli 2022, Motor Control — the field has "no consensus motor-control theory"). "Using reflexes to facilitate voluntary movement" is 1960s neurophysiology, not a contemporary framework. By extension, the broad SR-level finding (facilitation-based approaches not categorically superior to task-specific training) applies.

## Vested-interest signal

**Very low.** There is no active named-tutor commercial infrastructure equivalent to Bobath's. The name survives through a framework and a textbook, not a credentialing body — so there is little financial incentive structure to defend.

## UK NHS uptake

The **Brunnstrom Stages** are widely used as a descriptive recovery-staging tool (directly, and embedded in the FMA). **Brunnstrom Therapy as a treatment intervention** is rarely the named primary approach in UK NHS practice. NICE NG236 and RCP/ISWP 2023 name neither — guideline silence, neither recommended nor discouraged.

## Debate-mode handling

- **Split the claim every time:** Brunnstrom **Stages framework** (alive, embedded in FMA, no serious challenge) vs Brunnstrom **therapy as intervention** (thin direct evidence, theoretical headwinds). Never let one half stand for the other.
- **Hedge the therapy explicitly:** "very little direct evidence; here is what we know versus do not know." This honest uncertainty-flagging IS the behaviour, not a deficiency.
- **Present Pandian 2011 with its limits attached** — one small single-centre RCT favouring BHM > MRP for chronic hand recovery, but with sample, generalisability and measurement-bias caveats, and unreplicated. Do not let it overturn the broader SR-level pattern.
- **Name the absence-of-SR gap** — no Cochrane/major SR on Brunnstrom therapy as intervention appears to exist; that is itself a gap, not a verdict.
