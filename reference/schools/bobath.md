# Bobath Concept / Neuro-Developmental Treatment (NDT)

The most politically contested school in UK stroke rehabilitation, and the build's sharpest worked example. Read alongside `reference/debates/bobath-method-vs-framework.md` and `reference/debates/facilitation-as-mechanism.md`.

## Founder

Dr Karel Bobath (medical doctor) and Berta Bobath (physiotherapist), working as a clinical partnership. Jewish refugees from Germany who relocated to the UK in the 1930s.

## Origin

Developed from their London treatment centres starting in the 1940s and taught for roughly 50 years. UK-origin (London). Adopted in the US under the name **Neuro-Developmental Treatment (NDT)** — the NDT name was coined by Berta Bobath for the US transfer. The Bobath Centre London continues to operate (50th anniversary 2007).

## Core theoretical claim

Two distinct layers — the build must not collapse them:

- **Original (1940s–1960s):** Recovery from CNS damage is achievable by *inhibition* of abnormal tone and *facilitation* of normal movement patterns through the therapist's manual handling. The therapist's hands guide the patient's movement to elicit "normal" motor responses; abnormal synergies are inhibited. Roots in mid-20th-century reflex-hierarchical motor-control theory.
- **Contemporary (post-1990s revision):** Reframed as a problem-solving clinical-reasoning *framework* anchored on movement analysis plus individualised goal-setting — not a fixed technique. Mayston's 2008 editorial explicitly argues against treating Bobath as a fixed "approach" and toward treating it as a contributor to client-centred neurorehabilitation.

## Evidence stance

The systematic-review literature consistently finds Bobath **not superior** to other approaches, and **inferior** to task-specific training where directly compared. GRADE-calibrated: this is a robust, repeated SR-level finding.

- **Scrivener et al. 2020** (Australian Journal of Physiotherapy, PROSPERO CRD42019112451): SR + meta-analysis of 22 trials (17 in meta-analyses). Bobath therapy inferior to task-specific training for lower-limb activities (SMD 0.48, 95% CI 0.01 to 0.95). Not superior to combined interventions (SMD −0.06, 95% CI −0.73 to 0.61) or strength training (SMD 0.35, 95% CI −0.37 to 1.08). Conclusion: *"Prioritising Bobath therapy over other interventions is not supported by current evidence."* Trial PEDro range 2–8/10.
- **Kollen et al. 2009** (Stroke, AHA Journals): SR of 16 RCTs, best-evidence synthesis. Conclusion: *"This systematic review confirms that overall the Bobath Concept is not superior to other approaches. Based on best evidence synthesis, no evidence is available for the superiority of any approach."* Note: individual trials cut both ways — Platz Arm BASIS training beat Bobath on Fugl-Meyer arm score; Thaut rhythmic-auditory stimulation beat NDT/Bobath gait training; but one Wang orthopedic-vs-Bobath trial *favoured* Bobath on MAS/SIS.
- **Pathak et al. 2021** (J Family Med Prim Care, PROSPERO CRD42019125400): *"This study is inconclusive in determining the effectiveness of the Bobath approach."*
- **Pollock et al. 2014**: same SR-level conclusion — Bobath inferior to task-specific training, not superior to other interventions except PNF for standing balance.
- **Novak et al. 2013** (cerebral-palsy-focused): gave NDT/Bobath a "red light, don't do it" verdict that prompted EBTA's published rebuttal.
- **Load-bearing nuance:** no SR has tested the *framework-vs-treatment* distinction. Intervention trials treat Bobath as a treatment condition; they do not test the clinical-reasoning-framework claim. So the framework defence is not refutable by the intervention evidence — see `reference/debates/bobath-method-vs-framework.md`.

## UK named defenders

- **Margaret Mayston** — UCL Senior Teaching Fellow and Senior Bobath Tutor; worked with the Bobaths personally. Nuanced position: Bobath as a framework, not as an evidence-validated treatment. 2008 *Physiotherapy Research International* editorial "Bobath Concept: Bobath@50: mid-life crisis — What of the future?" is the canonical nuanced-defender source. (Living; first name Margaret.)
- **Gill Stern** — past President, European Bobath Tutors' Association (EBTA). Authored the formal EBTA response to Novak et al. 2013.
- **Nikki Penny** — UK neuro physiotherapist, ex-Bobath tutor track; declared no financial stake. Defends Bobath courses as movement-observation training, not as a treatment method.
- **Bobath Tutors UK / EBTA / IBITA** (International Bobath Instructors Training Association) — institutional bodies maintaining UK course delivery.

## UK named critics

- **Sarah Tyson** — UK neuro physiotherapy educator/researcher. On vested interests: *"Many have invested time, effort, finances and reputation on the named approaches... vested interests in keeping them going."*
- **Zack Siddells** — former UK neuro physiotherapy teacher: *"the more-or-less complete failure of trials investigating BB to show benefit. Yet a huge body of therapists just don't get it and continue to flog a dead horse."*
- **JB8205** — UK acute neuroscience-unit physiotherapist; first-person testimony of being an EBP-aligned physio in a Bobath-dominant team.
- **Ruud van der Veen** (Netherlands): *"Bobath was abandoned in the Netherlands in 2007 in favor of an evidence-based approach."*
- **Erik Prinsen** — evidence-based-facts-vs-experience framing.

## Vested-interest signal

**High** — the strongest of all six schools. Bobath has a named-course-tutor commercial credentialing structure (Bobath Tutor status is described as "a very lucrative place to be"). The vested interest cuts **both ways**: defenders hold commercial course-delivery and credential stakes; but critics can hold their own non-commercial stakes too — reputation and career investment in the competing evidence-based paradigm — so a critique is not automatically disinterested. When the educator's planned audience may have financial or status stakes in the answer, surface this dynamic proactively, in both directions.

## UK NHS uptake

Still widely practised in UK NHS stroke units; Davidson & Waters 2000 found 88% of UK neurological physiotherapists used the Bobath approach. JB8205's first-person account of being in the minority *as an EBP physio* implies local Bobath-dominance is the norm. Tyson & Selley 2007 found UK therapists "seemed to follow a traditional Bobath model in practice, despite their self-perception of being eclectic." Current UK guidelines (NICE NG236, RCP/ISWP 2023) do **not** name Bobath as a recommended approach — guideline language is intervention-named (RTT, CIMT, strength), not approach-named.

## Debate-mode handling

- **Always separate the two layers** — Bobath-as-treatment-intervention vs Bobath-as-movement-observation-framework. Surface the Mayston/Penny nuanced position. Never collapse "Bobath as movement-observation training" with "Bobath as evidence-validated treatment."
- **Name the fault lines:** facilitation-based (not task-oriented) on fault line 1; the treatment-vs-framework split on fault line 2.
- **State the SR convergence honestly:** four SRs across 16 years agree Bobath is not superior; it is inferior to task-specific training where directly compared. But it is not inferior on every outcome to every comparator.
- **Surface the vested-interest dynamic proactively** (cuts both ways) when stakes are present.
- **Name the genuine gap:** the framework-vs-treatment distinction is theoretical and not refutable by intervention trials. The "Bobath as framework" defence is un-falsifiable within current SR methodology because SRs test interventions, not frameworks.
