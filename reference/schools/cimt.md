# Constraint-Induced Movement Therapy (CIMT)

The school with the strongest single RCT (EXCITE) and the sharpest NHS-resource tension — robust evidence for eligible patients, but a 6-hours/day × 2-week protocol that standard NHS outpatient services rarely deliver. Read alongside `reference/debates/cimt-nhs-resource.md`.

## Founder

Edward Taub, psychologist and neuroscientist, University of Alabama at Birmingham (UAB).

## Origin

Original mechanism research in the 1960s–80s on deafferented primates (the "learned non-use" concept). Translation to human upper-extremity stroke rehabilitation from the late 1980s onward. US-origin (UAB), with worldwide uptake. UK NHS adoption is variable and eligibility-restricted (see below).

## Core theoretical claim

Stroke patients develop **"learned non-use"** of the impaired upper extremity — the brain learns to ignore the limb after initial paralysis even when motor capability partially returns. CIMT counteracts this by:

- **Restraining the unaffected limb** (a padded safety mitt worn roughly 90% of waking hours) for 2 weeks.
- **Massed practice** with the affected limb — around 6 hours/day of behavioural shaping and repetitive task practice from a library of ~60 graded tasks.
- **Behavioural transfer package** — strategies to transfer gains to home/community use.

This places CIMT on the **task-oriented** side of fault line 1, alongside Carr-Shepherd/MRP.

## Evidence stance

The best-evidenced school on this list for eligible patients. GRADE-calibrated: moderate-to-high for arm-hand activity and self-reported use in eligible patients; with two important precision points (mechanism is largely compensation; the timing window matters).

- **Wolf et al. 2006 — EXCITE trial** (JAMA; NCT00057018): multisite RCT, n=222 patients 3–9 months post-stroke. CIMT vs usual-and-customary care over 2 weeks. Statistically significant improvement on **all primary outcomes** — WMFT Performance Time (between-group difference ~34%, p<.001); MAL Amount of Use (1.21→2.13 vs 1.15→1.65, p<.001); MAL Quality of Movement (1.26→2.23 vs 1.18→1.66, p<.001); SIS Hand domain (p=.05). The first multisite RCT of a non-surgical/non-pharmacological intervention in this stroke population.
- **Wolf et al. 2010 — EXCITE follow-up** (Stroke): early (3–9 months) vs delayed (15–21 months) CIMT. Both groups improved significantly; the early group greater at 12 months; by 24 months no statistically significant difference between groups. CIMT works at both timepoints in this eligibility window.
- **Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015** (SR + meta-analysis, 51 RCTs, n=1784): strong evidence favouring **both** original CIMT and **mCIMT** (modified, lower-intensity-over-longer-duration) for motor function, arm-hand activities, and self-reported amount + quality of arm-hand use; effects sustained at ~21.6-week follow-up. **Forced Use alone** (mitt only, no shaping) showed **no added value** — the active ingredients are shaping + repetitive practice + behavioural-change instruction, not the constraint alone. No significant difference between original and modified CIMT in pooled analysis, **except** mCIMT's motor-function (FMA-arm) effect was restricted to trials begun within the first 3 months post-stroke. Mechanism finding: improvements are *"mainly based on adaptation by learning to optimize the use of intact end-effectors"* — i.e. largely **compensation-strategy learning, not neural repair** (except possibly in the first 3 months).
- **Eligibility window (load-bearing constraint):** CIMT requires active wrist + finger extension (commonly ≥20° wrist + ≥10° finger; lower-functioning variants ≥10° wrist + ≥10° thumb + ≥10° two-finger). Patients with very severe motor impairment do not meet eligibility criteria. About **10% (range 3–90%) of initially screened patients were eligible** across included trials.

## UK named defenders

No UK-specific named-clinician defenders surfaced. The defender base is Taub's UAB lab and the broad motor-learning research community internationally. In the UK, CIMT's standing is carried by guideline inclusion (with caveats) rather than a named faction.

## UK named critics

No named UK critic faction. The critique is **practical-implementation**, not personal: 6 hours/day for 2 weeks is high-resource and not feasible in standard NHS outpatient delivery (forced clinic attendance, supervision, mitt compliance). UK guideline bodies encode this caution — NICE NG236 §1.13.19 says only *"Consider"* CIMT (the weakest NICE strength term) with eligibility criteria and adverse-event flags (falls, low mood, fatigue). Modified-CIMT (mCIMT) protocols emerged to address the resource barrier; their evidence base is treated together with CIMT in Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015 but the dose/intensity trade-offs matter clinically.

## Vested-interest signal

**Lower than Bobath.** UAB licenses CIMT-Plus training programmes (a moderate institutional commercial stake), but CIMT is not politically contested in UK debate and has no named-tutor-association equivalent to EBTA / Bobath Tutors UK.

## UK NHS uptake

**Variable.** Eligibility criteria plus the 6-hour/day resource demand limit routine NHS delivery. mCIMT is more practical for NHS settings, but its evidence is read jointly with standard CIMT and carries its own timing caveat. NICE NG236 and RCP/ISWP 2023 both say *"Consider"* CIMT for eligible patients; ESO 2023 *"Strongly recommends"* it — that cross-jurisdictional disagreement is itself part of the debate (see `reference/debates/cimt-nhs-resource.md`).

## Debate-mode handling

- **Place it as task-oriented** on fault line 1.
- **Lead with EXCITE** as the strongest single trial, but immediately attach the two precision points: the mechanism is *largely compensation, not neural repair* (Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015), and the *eligibility window is narrow* (~10% of screened patients).
- **Surface the NHS-resource tension explicitly** — the evidence is good but the standard protocol (6 hr/day × 2 weeks) rarely fits NHS outpatient delivery; mCIMT is the practical alternative, with the <3-month timing caveat for motor-function gains.
- **Name the cross-jurisdictional disagreement** (NICE/RCP "Consider" vs ESO "Strongly recommend") and geography-flag the non-UK source per the UK-primary rule.
- **Note that Forced Use alone does not work** — pre-empt the misconception that the mitt is the active ingredient.
