# Debate: Task-specific training vs eclectic mix

The contested claim: *"Task-specific / repetitive task training (TST/RTT) is the recommended primary approach to stroke motor rehabilitation; named-approach eclecticism (Bobath + Carr-Shepherd + PNF + Brunnstrom mixed by clinician preference) is sub-optimal"* — vs — *"UK clinicians can and do switch between named approaches based on patient presentation and clinical experience; the guideline-recommended interventions are one input among many."* Spans `reference/schools/carr-shepherd.md`, `reference/schools/cimt.md`, and touches every facilitation-based school. This is the **guideline-level** debate — the one most cleanly resolved at the recommendation level, but with a subtle false-dichotomy underneath.

## For

TST/RTT as the primary approach — what UK + ESO 2023 guidelines actually say. The killer artefact is **Olaoye, Lokesh, Aboderin & Ojo 2024** (Healthcare 12:1433), a 3-way comparison of NICE NG236, RCP/ISWP 2023, and ESO 2023:

| Approach | NICE NG236 | RCP/ISWP 2023 | ESO 2023 (geography-flagged) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Therapeutic intensity | Offer min 3 hr therapy on ≥5 days/week | Same | Strongly recommend as much as possible |
| Strength training | Consider | Offer | Strongly recommend |
| **Task-specific / repetitive task training** | **Offer RTT/TST** | **Offer RTT/TST** | **Strongly recommend RTT/TST** |
| Mental practice | No recommendation | Offer as adjunct | Strongly recommend as adjunct |

- **All three guidelines AGREE** on TST/RTT.
- **RCP/ISWP positions it:** *"RTT should be the principal rehabilitation method alongside traditional exercise."* RTT defined as *"intensive, task-specific, functional and consisting of a high number of repetitions."*
- **NICE NG236 §1.13.20:** *"Offer people after stroke repetitive task training on a range of activities for upper limb weakness... and lower limb weakness (such as sit-to-stand transfers, walking and using stairs)."*
- **No named approaches (Bobath / Carr-Shepherd / PNF / Brunnstrom / Peto) appear in NICE NG236 or RCP/ISWP 2023.** Guideline language is **intervention-named** (CIMT, RTT, strength training, electrical stimulation, mirror therapy), not approach-named — a deliberate consolidation.

## Against

Eclectic-mix as clinically realistic — but read carefully, the named voices actually *qualify* the "eclectic" defence:

- **Tyson & Selley 2007** (UK survey): UK therapists *"seemed to follow a traditional Bobath model in practice, despite their self-perception of being eclectic."* This **undercuts** the eclectic-mix defence — therapists describe themselves as eclectic but practice patterns reveal a tradition-lean.
- **The contractor's own observation** (`research/user-profile-investigation.md`): UK clinicians "cheerfully switch between schools" based on patient — first-person UK clinical-practice testimony that the eclectic pattern is real.
- **Mayston 2008** is **against** approach-based eclecticism and **for** intervention-based individualisation: *"an integrated approach to neurorehabilitation that is not based on approaches, but rather is client based."*
- **RCP/ISWP 2023** frames personalisation as guideline-recommended (*"Personalised rehabilitation types and intensity... offered by a range of multidisciplinary team members"*) — i.e. *intervention-level* personalisation, not named-approach mixing.

## Evidence

- **Guideline level (NICE NG236 + RCP/ISWP 2023 + ESO 2023):** TST/RTT universally recommended as the principal rehabilitation method; named approaches not endorsed by name.
- **SR level:** task-specific training outperforms Bobath in direct comparisons (Pollock 2014; Scrivener 2020 SMD 0.48; Platz Arm BASIS > Bobath and Thaut RAS > NDT/Bobath gait training within Kollen 2009). No SR endorses approach-switching as a clinical strategy.
- **Practice-pattern level:** UK clinicians describe themselves as eclectic but practice shows tradition-lean (Tyson & Selley 2007) — the eclectic-mix description may be more cultural than empirically grounded.
- **Motor-control theory level (Levin & Piscitelli 2022):** no unified theory grounds eclectic-approach-mixing; the theoretical headwinds favour intervention-based, not approach-based, practice.
- **The nuance the build must surface:** clinical practice IS often eclectic, but the *guideline-recommended* version of "eclectic" is **intervention-level personalisation grounded in RTT + strength + CIMT-when-eligible + adjuncts**, NOT **named-approach switching between Bobath / Carr-Shepherd / Brunnstrom on clinical preference.** The two look similar in practice but are theoretically distinct positions — much of the apparent disagreement is a false dichotomy at the guideline level.

## Vested-interest

- **Guideline bodies (NICE, RCP/ISWP, ESO)** have an institutional incentive to consolidate around evidence-based interventions — that is their job, the *output* of the guideline process, not a hidden bias.
- **Bobath / PNF / Brunnstrom credential bodies** have a vested interest in the eclectic-mix framing because it preserves named-approach courses as legitimate CPD content.

## UK anchor
