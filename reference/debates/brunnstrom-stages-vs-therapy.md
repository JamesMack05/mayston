# Debate: Brunnstrom stages framework vs Brunnstrom therapy

The contested claim: *"Brunnstrom therapy (using synergies and reflexes to facilitate voluntary motor control) is a viable contemporary stroke rehabilitation intervention"* — vs — *"Brunnstrom therapy as an intervention is largely superseded; only the Brunnstrom Stages framework (as a staging tool) retains contemporary utility."* This debate has a different shape from the others — **both positions can be partly true**, and the resolution is cleaner than for Bobath. Spans `reference/schools/brunnstrom.md`.

## For

Two sub-positions to keep distinct:

**(a) Brunnstrom therapy as a contemporary intervention** — the named direct-RCT defender:
- **Pandian, Arya & Davidson 2011** (J Bodywork and Movement Therapies 16(3):330-337, PMID 22703742; Pt. Deendayal Upadhyaya Institute, University of Delhi). RCT n=30 chronic post-stroke. Group A: Brunnstrom Hand Manipulation (BHM, "uses synergies and reflexes to develop voluntary motor control"). Group B: MRP hand protocol. Both effective (BRS-H p=0.003–0.004; FMA-WH p<0.001). **BHM > MRP for FMA-WH (p<0.004) and FMA item VIII hand motor recovery (p<0.033).** Conclusion: *"BHM was found to be more effective than MRP in rehabilitation of the hand in chronic post-stroke patients."* This directly contradicts the broad SR-level message ("task-specific beats facilitation-based") for the specific case of chronic-phase hand recovery — worth surfacing, with limits.

**(b) Brunnstrom Stages framework as a staging tool** — strongly defended:
- **Gladstone et al. 2002** (PMID 12234086): the Fugl-Meyer Assessment, whose seven-stage motor-recovery structure derives directly from the Brunnstrom framework, *"is recommended highly as a clinical and research tool for evaluating changes in motor impairment."* The framework is embedded in mainstream assessment.
- **Hsieh et al. 2016** — work to "improve the utility of the Brunnstrom recovery stages" confirms the staging tool persists even where the therapy is not used.

## Against

Brunnstrom *therapy* as an intervention is superseded:

- **Physiopedia 2023:** *"There are several methods on rehabilitation of stroke patients however very little evidence is present"* for Brunnstrom therapy.
- **Levin & Piscitelli 2022** (theoretical objection): Brunnstrom's foundational reflex-hierarchical motor-control theory is contested at the motor-control-theory level; reflex-and-synergy-driven recovery is mid-20th-century neurophysiology, and current motor-learning literature does not support reflex-driven facilitation as causally separable from practice intensity.
- **Indirect SR evidence:** Kollen 2009, Pollock 2014, Scrivener 2020 find no consistent superiority of facilitation-based approaches over task-specific training; by extension (Brunnstrom is facilitation-based), the general SR-level evidence does not support Brunnstrom therapy as a primary intervention.
- **No major contemporary SR specifically on Brunnstrom therapy as treatment** was located — the thin-evidence gap is itself the position.

(Nobody contests sub-position (b): the Stages framework as a staging tool is not seriously challenged.)

## Evidence

- **Stages framework: alive and well.** Embedded in the FMA, widely used, no serious challenge to its descriptive utility as a recovery-staging tool.
- **Brunnstrom therapy as intervention: thin evidence + theoretical headwinds.** Pandian 2011 is one small RCT (BHM > MRP for chronic hand recovery), but **generalisability and the broad SR-level pattern constrain interpretation.** Pandian 2011 limitations: n=30 (15/arm); single-centre Indian institute (UK NHS generalisability not established); BRS-H outcome derives from the Brunnstrom framework itself (potential measurement bias); not replicated in the retrievable literature.
- **The build's honest position:** the framework-vs-therapy distinction in Brunnstrom mirrors the framework-vs-treatment distinction in Bobath — but it is **more clearly resolved** here (framework retained; therapy thin) than for Bobath (still actively contested).

## Vested-interest

**Very low.** No commercial Brunnstrom-tutor infrastructure equivalent to Bobath / EBTA. The name persists via the Stages framework and the textbook ("Movement Therapy in Hemiplegia"), not a credentialing body — so little incentive structure to defend.

## UK anchor

No UK-specific Brunnstrom institution or training body identified. NICE NG236 and RCP/ISWP 2023 name Brunnstrom Therapy neither as recommended nor as discouraged — guideline silence. The FMA (Brunnstrom-derived) is in routine UK assessment use, which is the indirect UK anchor for sub-position (b).

## Named gaps

- **Pandian 2011 generalisability to a UK NHS chronic-stroke caseload** not established.
- **Replication studies of Pandian 2011** not retrieved.
- **A Cochrane / major SR specifically on Brunnstrom therapy as intervention** does not appear to exist — absence-of-evidence on the SR side itself, distinct from evidence-of-absence.
