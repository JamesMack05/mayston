# Debate: Facilitation — real neurophysiological mechanism or therapist-construct?

The contested claim: *"Facilitation (manual handling by the therapist to elicit a more normal movement response) is a real neurophysiological mechanism"* — vs — *"Facilitation is a construct in the mind of its proponents; the apparent effects are explained by biomechanical / muscle-length / motor-learning / attention / observer-bias processes, with no specific neurophysiological mechanism established."* Spans `reference/schools/bobath.md`, `reference/schools/pnf.md`, and `reference/schools/brunnstrom.md` — every facilitation-based school rests on this premise.

## For

Facilitation as a real mechanism:

- **Berta Bobath** (1970, 1978, 1990 textbooks) — introduced "inhibition" and "facilitation" as physiological explanations for the effects of handling.
- **EBTA / Bobath Tutors UK** — institutional position: specialised handling reduces hypertonia and is not passive; *"to describe Bobath as 'passive handling'... is inaccurate."*
- The original theoretical basis was the **reflex-hierarchical motor-control model** (Sherrington reflex theory, Foerster hierarchical CNS organisation) — the mid-20th-century neurophysiology in which facilitation made sense.
- **Clinical-experience reports** of immediate change in movement quality after therapist handling — e.g. Penny's account of Bobath courses as movement courses that let her "begin to understand movement... see an experienced clinician identify movement problems."

Note: the "for" position's strongest surviving form is really the *framework / clinical-utility* claim (Debate: bobath-method-vs-framework), not a mechanism claim.

## Against

Facilitation as a construct:

- **Mayston 2008 herself** — the leading UK Bobath tutor **retracts the original mechanism claim**: *"the word 'inhibition' is no longer a relevant explanation for the ways Bobath therapists stretch and activate muscles"*; and *"suggesting inhibition is a physiological explanation for what therapists achieve by handling is not scientifically correct. Therapists are effecting changes in both inhibitory and excitatory synapses simultaneously, but their 'inhibition' also affects visco-elastic properties of muscle and by improving muscle length can gain a better biomechanical advantage."* A defender conceding the mechanism is not what the Bobaths claimed.
- **Levin & Piscitelli 2022** (Motor Control) — critique at the motor-control-theory level: the field uses "a hodgepodge of different models, hypotheses, principles, and theories"; there is **no consensus motor-control theory** underlying neurorehabilitation; the "direct framework" underlying facilitation-based approaches has "several limitations... physiologically incompatible with the nonlinear properties of motoneurons." The foundation facilitation rests on is itself contested.
- **The intervention-level SR record** (Pollock 2014, Scrivener 2020, Pathak 2021, Kollen 2009) — none demonstrate facilitation-based approaches outperform task-specific training; the evidence does not support facilitation as a causally load-bearing mechanism above practice intensity, task-specificity and attention.

## Evidence

- **No SR or meta-analysis has demonstrated facilitation-as-mechanism** as causally separable from practice intensity, task-specificity, attention, and biomechanical effects.
- **The most authoritative defender (Mayston) has retracted the original mechanism claim** while continuing to defend Bobath practice on framework/clinical-utility grounds — so the mechanism debate and the framework debate must be kept separate.
- **The broader motor-control landscape (Levin & Piscitelli 2022)** offers no unified neurophysiological model on which facilitation could rest.
- **What remains** is the empirical observation that Bobath-trained therapists report clinically-useful movement-observation skills — which is the *framework* claim, not the *mechanism* claim.
- **Critics' alternative explanations for what happens when "facilitation" appears to work:** biomechanical/muscle-length change from stretch; motor learning from practice with manual/verbal/visual prompting (the manual prompt being one cue among many, not specifically causal); therapist attention / non-specific effects; observer bias and interpersonal-response effects.

## Vested-interest

- The institutional vested interest from the Bobath debate applies specifically here, because **facilitation is a load-bearing course-content item** for Bobath / NDT (and PNF) credentialing — conceding the mechanism undercuts the course content.
- **Levin & Piscitelli** are McGill motor-control academics with academic-prestige incentive but no commercial stake in any therapeutic school.

## UK anchor

Mayston @ UCL — the load-bearing UK source *acknowledging the mechanism claim is retracted*. The contemporary UK clinical voice names the conflict directly: the contractor's own framing that "the evidence says facilitation is not a thing, that it's a construct in the mind of the proponents."

## Named gaps

- **Direct comparative studies of facilitation vs no-facilitation under matched practice conditions** were not retrieved — would need a design holding practice constant and varying only therapist hands-on facilitation.
- **Qualitative research on therapist beliefs about facilitation** (Lennon-type studies) not deeply retrieved — could sharpen the "construct in the therapist's mind" claim with primary qualitative evidence.
- **Specific facilitation-mechanism critiques** (Pellecchia-type) not retrieved.
