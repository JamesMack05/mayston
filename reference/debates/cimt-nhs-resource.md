# Debate: CIMT in NHS-resourced settings (standard vs modified)

The contested claim: *"Constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT) should be a standard NHS upper-limb intervention for eligible chronic stroke patients despite its high-intensity protocol (6 hr/day × 2 weeks)"* — vs — *"Standard CIMT is impractical for routine NHS delivery; modified CIMT (mCIMT) is an acceptable alternative, but the evidence base and clinical trade-offs differ."* Spans `reference/schools/cimt.md`.

## For

CIMT and mCIMT are robust; the NHS should deliver:

- **Edward Taub** (originator, UAB) + **Wolf et al.** (EXCITE trial, JAMA 2006).
- **European Stroke Organisation (ESO) 2023 guidelines** — *strongly recommend* traditional or modified CIMT (the strongest of the three guideline bodies; ESO imposes no qualifying motor-function criteria). **Geography-flag: ESO is a non-UK secondary source.**

The "for" case: EXCITE showed significant medium effect sizes for arm-hand activities and self-reported amount + quality of arm-hand use, sustained at follow-up; the pooled meta-analysis (Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015) supports both original and modified CIMT.

## Against

Practical NHS-resource critique plus nuance on what actually works:

- **NICE NG236 2023 (§1.13.19):** *"Consider* constraint-induced movement therapy for people after stroke who have movement of 20 degrees of wrist extension and 10 degrees of finger extension. Be aware of potential adverse events (such as falls, low mood and fatigue)." *"Consider"* is the **weakest** NICE strength term (below "Offer"/"Recommend").
- **RCP / Intercollegiate Stroke Working Party 2023:** same *"Consider"* language and eligibility criteria; separately recommends repetitive task training more strongly as the principal rehabilitation method.
- **Implementation-feasibility critique:** 6 hr/day × 2 weeks is a delivery model NHS outpatient stroke services rarely provide (forced clinic attendance, supervision, mitt compliance). mCIMT is workable but its dose-response evidence is mixed.

## Evidence

- **Kwakkel & Veerbeek 2015** (SR + meta-analysis, 51 RCTs, n=1784): strong evidence favouring **both** original CIMT and mCIMT for motor function, arm-hand activities, and self-reported amount + quality of arm-hand use; effects sustained ~21.6 weeks. **No significant difference** between original and modified CIMT, dose, or timing in pooled analysis — **except** mCIMT's motor-function (FMA-arm) effect was restricted to trials begun **within the first 3 months** post-stroke. **Forced Use alone** (mitt, no shaping): **no added value** — the active ingredients are shaping + repetitive practice + behavioural-change instruction, not the constraint. Mechanism: improvements are *"mainly based on adaptation by learning to optimize the use of intact end-effectors"* — i.e. **compensation-strategy learning, not neural repair** (except possibly in the first 3 months).
- **Eligibility narrowness:** about **10% (range 3–90%)** of initially screened patients were eligible across included trials. CIMT serves a narrow eligibility subset — a structural constraint on its NHS service-design role.
- **Not categorically superior:** the Suputtitada trial (within Kollen 2009) — CIMT vs Bobath in chronic stroke (n=69) — found *"No statistically significant differences... for ARAT, hand grip, and pinch strength."*
- **The cross-jurisdictional disagreement IS the debate:** NICE NG236 + RCP "Consider" (eligibility-restricted, adverse-event-flagged) vs ESO "Strongly recommend." UK-primary, ESO geography-flagged.

## Vested-interest

- **UAB Taub-lab licensing** of CIMT-Plus training programmes — a moderate institutional commercial stake.
- **Less politically loaded than Bobath** in the UK NHS context — no named tutor-association equivalent to EBTA / Bobath Tutors UK.

## UK anchor

NICE NG236 §1.13.19 (England + Wales); RCP / Intercollegiate Stroke Working Party National Clinical Guideline for Stroke 2023 (UK + Ireland); Olaoye et al. 2024 (Healthcare 12:1433) — a UK-affiliated review explicitly comparing the UK and ESO guidelines. ESO 2023 is the non-UK secondary anchor and must be geography-flagged.
