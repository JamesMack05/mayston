# Comp 6 — Dad follow-up call

**Source:** Phone call with Dad. Follow-up to the first call (`user-profile-investigation.md`). A short call to check UK CPD course content, source-access reality, and the contemporary stroke-rehab schools scene against his lived experience.

---

## Q1 — CPD course content: named approaches vs intervention-language

> *"In the CPD courses you or your colleagues have actually attended in the last year or two — are they still framed around named approaches (e.g. 'come learn the Bobath approach', 'come learn PNF'), or have they shifted to intervention-language ('task-specific upper-limb training', 'repetitive task training')? Give me 2-3 concrete examples."*

Task specific is everything, and that's what they call it. Supports their arm, and they start to try to move their arm doing that, and that's good if they've already got some, not much help if they haven't.

This is half of the challenge: now what's being taught is so simplistic. The last part of the course, an evening course that I went on, was by seven or eight of the most experienced physios, and they are discussing that the exercise needs to be quite vigorous to be the best. We got the most experienced people back to stating the obvious and calling it training.

## Q2 — CINAHL / Athens access reality

> *"When you work across different NHS Trusts on contracts — does your Athens login (CINAHL, BMJ full-text, Cochrane full-text) persist between contracts, or drop when each contract ends? And as a community physio mostly working outside a Trust, do you have working Athens access, or default to free sources?"*

Through the chartered society, but not a lot. I haven't really tested it, to be honest. I tend to revert to using AI tools like Elicit for retrieval. Elicit will source roughly speaking top 10 most cited articles, collate them, and summarise for me.

## Q4 — Johnstone approach

> *"Last call you mentioned 'Johnston' alongside Bobath / PNF / Brunnstrom / Peto. Was that Margaret Johnstone — the Scottish PT, pressure air-splints — or someone else? Is the approach still taught or used in UK practice?"*

Yes, was Margaret Johnstone. Relatable: slings and weight — she called it weight-bearing. I call it weight-taking techniques to restore muscle tone and function.
